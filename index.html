🧠 How the Optimization Works

DynaShift AI uses a multi-objective constraint-satisfaction approach to build and repair workforce schedules in real time. It balances three competing goals simultaneously: maximum coverage, schedule stability, and fair workload distribution.

🔒

Hard Constraints

These must never be violated. Any schedule that breaks a hard constraint is immediately penalised heavily in the fitness function.

  • ✅ Availability — employees are only assigned to shifts they are available for
  • ✅ Skill match — shifts requiring specific skills only assign qualified employees
  • ✅ Staffing demand — each shift slot must meet the minimum headcount
  • ✅ Max consecutive days — no employee works more than N days in a row
  • ✅ Max hours/week — weekly hours stay within the contract limit
🎯

Soft Objectives

These are optimised but can be traded off against each other. They are weighted and summed into a single fitness score.

  • ⚖️ Fairness — minimise standard deviation of shifts per employee
  • 🔄 Minimum change — after a disruption, change as few assignments as possible
  • 📊 Workload balance — distribute shifts evenly across the team
  • 📈 Coverage surplus — avoid over-staffing as well as under-staffing
📐

Minimum-Change Metric

After a disruption the repair engine starts from a copy of the original schedule and only modifies what is necessary:

  1. Remove all assignments that are now invalid (employee absent / unavailable)
  2. Fill under-covered slots by selecting the least-loaded available substitute
  3. Trim over-covered slots by releasing the most-loaded employee
  4. Repeat for up to 50 iterations until no improvement is possible

Change Cost = ∑ cells where revised[e][d] ≠ initial[e][d]

🔬

Algorithms

GA
Genetic Algorithm — population of 20 schedules evolved over up to 200 generations. Tournament selection, single-point crossover, random mutation. Seeded with a greedy solution for fast convergence.
SA
Simulated Annealing — starts from a greedy solution, randomly perturbs one slot per step, accepts worse solutions with probability e^(−Δ/T). Temperature decays at α=0.995.
CP
Constraint Propagation — builds per-employee domains, applies arc-consistency to enforce max-hours, assigns greedily using the MRV (Minimum Remaining Values) heuristic.
GR
Greedy Heuristic — iterates days and shifts, always assigning the least-loaded available employee first. Fast O(n·d·s) baseline used to seed the other algorithms.
🏋️

Fitness Function

Lower total score = better schedule. The three components are independently weighted via the sliders on the left panel.

Total=wcov × CoveragePenalty
+wfair × FairnessPenalty
+1.0 × ConstraintPenalty

💥

Disruption Types

👤
Employee Absence — marks 1–3 employees as inactive, zeroes their availability for the affected day. All their pre-assigned slots become conflicts.
📈
Demand Surge — multiplies staffing demand on the affected day by 1.25×, 1.5×, or 2× (low/medium/high). Creates immediate under-coverage.
🩹
Skill Loss / Injury — removes one skill from a randomly selected employee. Any shift that required that skill is now uncovered by that employee.
🔧
Equipment Failure — blocks an entire shift slot on the affected day for all employees. Simulates a facility or equipment outage.
